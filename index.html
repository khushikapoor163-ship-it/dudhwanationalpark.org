The Destination

India's last wild Terai

Before you pick a trip, know the place you're heading into.

Dudhwa Tiger Reserve sits on the Himalayan foothills along the Indo-Nepal border in Uttar Pradesh — the last major remnant of the Terai ecosystem, a mosaic of dense sal forest, tall grassland and wetland habitats.

It shelters Bengal tigers, reintroduced one-horned rhinos, swamp deer (barasingha), gharials and otters, alongside 400+ recorded bird species. The reserve is made up of three connected forest blocks — the Dudhwa core zone, Kishanpur Wildlife Sanctuary and Katerniaghat Wildlife Sanctuary — together forming one of the most biodiverse corners of northern India.

The park is typically open from mid-November to mid-June and closed through the monsoon months, when the Terai's rivers run full and its trails are impassable.

Getting There

How to reach Dudhwa

Plan the journey in — by air, by rail, or by road.

By Air

Nearest major airport is Lucknow (Chaudhary Charan Singh International Airport), roughly a 5–6 hour drive to the reserve.

By Rail

Nearest railheads are Dudhwa and Palia stations, both on the broad-gauge line connecting to Lucknow and Delhi.

By Road

Well-connected by state highway from Lucknow and Delhi; we can arrange pickup from Lucknow, Palia or Dudhwa on request.
